How to Choose a Post-Workout Shake

Protein, timing, and the boosters that actually matter

Choose a post-workout shake by its ingredients, serving size and how it fits your day. Protein and carbohydrates both have roles in sports nutrition, but a shake is a convenient option rather than a requirement for every workout.

Start with the finished drink

Check how much protein the prepared serving contains, what else is in it and whether that suits the meal or snack you need. The NIH Office of Dietary Supplements describes adequate food and hydration as the foundation for athletic performance. A supplement does not replace that foundation.

  • Protein: compare the amount in the complete serving.
  • Carbohydrates: consider your training and other meals.
  • Ingredients: check allergens and any optional additions.
  • Convenience: choose something you enjoy and can fit into your routine.

Do you need BCAAs or creatine?

Optional boosters are not a requirement for every shake. NIH describes limited evidence for some BCAA performance claims. Creatine has better support for certain repeated, high-intensity activities, but that does not establish that an unspecified scoop or combination will improve your recovery. If considering an addition, check the product, serving amount and whether it fits your needs.

What about timing?

Plan your shake alongside your usual meals and training schedule. A drink after exercise can be convenient, but the whole day's food intake matters too. Avoid treating a single drink or a rigid countdown as the entire recovery plan.

Common questions

Do I need a shake after every workout?

No. A shake can be convenient, but your usual meals can also contribute to your nutrition needs. Choose based on your overall food intake and training.

Should every post-workout shake include a booster?

No. Optional additions should be evaluated by their ingredients, serving amounts and your needs. They are not necessary for every drink.

How do I compare two protein shakes?

Compare the finished serving: protein, carbohydrates, calories, ingredients and additions. Powder-label values may not describe the complete prepared drink.
